Subtitles are the first choice you make when learning Japanese from anime, and the easiest one to get wrong. Plenty of people watch with subtitles in their own language for years and their Japanese barely moves; others jump straight to no subtitles and give up after two episodes. This page explains what each kind of subtitle trains, and how to move in stages from leaning on subtitles to leaning on your ears. It uses Japanese as the target language and calls the other one your "native language" — the logic is the same for any pair.

## Why don't native-language subtitles teach you much?

The problem isn't the subtitles, it's your eyes: **as long as there's a line on screen in a language you read fluently, you'll read it.** Reading your own language is far easier than listening to Japanese, so the brain takes the easy path. You end up watching a show in your own language that happens to have a Japanese audio track, and the Japanese is background noise.

That's why immersion advice usually recommends against native-language subtitles — you don't learn much from them. But "not recommended" doesn't mean "never": when you're completely lost and can't keep watching, a translation can be your safety net. The key is to have it appear only when you need it, not all the time.

## What do Japanese, dual and no subtitles each train?

| Subtitles | What you mainly practise | Upside | Downside |
|---|---|---|---|
| Native language | Little beyond following the plot | You follow the story completely | Your attention goes to your own language |
| Dual (Japanese + native shown together) | Side-by-side reading | You can match "this Japanese line means that" | The translation is right there; hard not to read it |
| Japanese | Reading + listening | Tap to look up and mine; the most input | Hard to keep up with and understand at first |
| None | Pure listening | Closest to real-life use | No way to look up words you can't catch |

Japanese subtitles are the workhorse early in immersion: you see how every word is written, you can tap unknown words to look them up and mine the ones worth keeping. While your listening is weak, the subtitles help you connect sounds to words; as reading gets easier you'll naturally start to "hear it first, then confirm with the subtitle".

Fan-sub "dual" ASS files often put both languages in one track, so you can't blur just the translation. To get "Japanese always visible, translation on demand", it's best to have Japanese and the translation as two separate subtitle tracks — one primary, one secondary.

## Stage by stage: from Japanese subtitles to none

The route below is a guide, not a rule. Being at stage 1 for the first few episodes of a show and naturally reaching stage 2 later on is normal, and so is stepping back when you switch to a harder show.

**Stage 1: just starting, understanding almost nothing**

- Primary subtitle: Japanese, shown.
- Secondary subtitle: your language, **blurred**; hover or tap to glance at it when stuck.
- What you do: follow the plot with Japanese subtitles and lookups, ignoring the translation most of the time.

**Stage 2: roughly following the plot**

- Primary subtitle: Japanese, shown.
- Secondary subtitle: **hidden**, or not loaded at all.
- What you do: rely on the Japanese subtitles only. Look up unknown words and mine the worthwhile ones (see [What is sentence mining?](/faq/sentence-mining.en)). This is the stage with the most input and it lasts the longest — no need to rush out of it.

**Stage 3: Japanese subtitles read smoothly**

- Primary subtitle: Japanese, **blurred**, revealed when paused or hovered.
- What you do: listen first, check the subtitle when you can't catch something. This is where listening practice really starts.

**Stage 4: listening is comfortable**

- Primary subtitle: **hidden**.
- What you do: pure listening, opening the subtitle list now and then to check a line.

When should you move down a stage? A simple test: **if you notice you rarely use the support from the current stage, remove it.** Conversely, if a new show or genre (say, from slice-of-life to mystery) leaves you completely lost, going back a stage is nothing to be ashamed of.

## How to set up subtitles in Fushi

- **Load a secondary subtitle**: in the player's subtitle menu there's a "Secondary subtitle" entry besides the primary one; pick a track in another language (an external file next to the video or an embedded track both work). The secondary subtitle has its own position and timing offset — see [How do I use a secondary subtitle as a bridge?](/faq/secondary-subtitle.en)
- **Blur / hide**: under **Video settings → Subtitles**, "Obscure subtitles" for the primary track and "Obscure secondary subtitle" for the secondary each offer Off / Blur / Hide.
- **Showing when paused**: with "Reveal when paused or hovered" on, a blurred or hidden subtitle shows temporarily when you pause, look up, hover (desktop) or tap (mobile); turn it off to keep it obscured even when paused.
- **Quick toggles**: during playback, the shortcuts "Cycle subtitle obscure mode", "Cycle secondary subtitle obscure" and "Toggle hide secondary subtitle" switch on the fly without opening settings.
- **Revisit a line**: open the "Subtitle list" to see the whole episode's dialogue and jump to any line.

## What if there are no Japanese subtitles?

Japanese subtitles can be hard to find, especially for older or less popular shows. Things to try:

- "**Search subtitles online**" in the subtitle menu: looks for Japanese subtitles on Jimaku by title (you'll need to enter a free Jimaku API key in settings);
- "**Generate subtitles with speech recognition**" in the subtitle menu: transcribes the audio track on your device into text subtitles you can look up and mine from (it needs the whole audio track: local videos and Interconnect remote videos work, other online streams don't);
- if the timing is off, see [What if subtitles are out of sync?](/faq/subtitle-sync.en)

## Common questions

- **Aren't dual subtitles the best of both worlds?** They help when you want to compare, but as your default they share the problem of native-language subtitles: the translation is right in front of you, so you'll read it. Better: Japanese always visible, translation blurred until you need it.
- **Doesn't going straight to no subtitles make you progress faster?** Only if you already understand most of it. Input you can't understand at all doesn't turn into ability, and makes giving up more likely. Starting with Japanese subtitles and gradually removing support is easier to sustain.
- **What about rewatching a show I've already seen in my own language?** You know the plot, so you can be one stage bolder than usual — Japanese subtitles only, or even blurred Japanese subtitles.
- **Learning a language other than Japanese?** Replace "Japanese" in the table with your target language; the logic is the same. Fushi supports many target languages — see [Is it only for Japanese?](/faq/languages.en)
